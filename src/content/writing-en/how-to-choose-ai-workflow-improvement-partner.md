When choosing a partner for AI workflow improvement, the practical question is **not "which AI can they use?" but "can they organize the workflow, validate it at small scale, implement it, and measure the result?"**

The seven points worth checking are:

1. Do they start from the current workflow rather than from AI?
2. Do they define PoC success and stop criteria?
3. Can they carry the work through to implementation when needed?
4. Do they measure accuracy, time, and cost?
5. Can they explain security and data boundaries?
6. Do they design where human review and approval remain?
7. Do they avoid locking you into a specific AI model?

Company size or name recognition alone won't tell you any of this. In the first meeting, confirm **what they will investigate first and what they will hand back as deliverables.**

## What are the seven criteria for comparing AI workflow improvement partners?

| Criterion | Question to ask | Warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow analysis | Do you review the current workflow first? | Talks only about products and AI models from the start |
| PoC design | How will success or failure be judged? | "Let's just build it" with no evaluation metric |
| Implementation | Can you handle implementation after validation? | Proposal and implementation are completely disconnected |
| Evaluation | How do you measure time saved, accuracy, and cost? | Treats a working demo as the only measure of success |
| Security | Which data goes where? | Cannot explain the data flow |
| Human-in-the-loop | Which steps are reviewed by a person? | Automates important decisions unconditionally |
| Portability | What must be rebuilt if the model changes? | The scope of dependency on a specific service is unclear |

## How do AI consultants differ from AI development companies?

In general, AI consultants focus on upstream work — deciding what to improve and in what order — while AI development companies focus on building things that run, such as PoCs and business systems.

In practice, many companies cross that line. So compare them **by what comes out as deliverables after the contract**, not by a company name or a "consulting" or "development" label.

| Partner type | Main deliverables | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI adoption consultant | Problem framing, priorities, roadmap, PoC plan | You have not decided what to apply AI to |
| AI development company | PoC, business tools, system integration, production implementation | What you want to build is relatively clear |
| Company covering assessment through implementation | Workflow analysis, validation plan, PoC, implementation when needed | You want to validate small before deciding on investment |

## Why not choose based only on which AI models they can use?

Models matter, but they alone do not decide whether workflow improvement succeeds.

With the same generative AI, results change depending on input data, retrieval method, permissions, prompts, evaluation, human review, and integration with existing systems. Whether the design lets you switch when a better-suited model appears also affects operating cost.

When ordering, ask not only "Will you use Claude, OpenAI, or Gemini?" but also **"If we change that model, how much has to be rebuilt?"** That makes the scope of dependency visible.

## What should be decided before building a PoC?

Before starting a PoC, decide at least:

- The target workflow
- The time or workload it currently takes
- The steps delegated to AI
- The steps reviewed by a person
- The correct answer or acceptable range
- The validation period
- The data to be used
- Success criteria
- Stop criteria

"The AI returned a plausible answer" is not enough to judge whether the workflow improved.

For customer inquiries, for example, an evaluation closer to real operations looks not only at answer accuracy but also at response time, how much staff have to correct, the risk of a wrong answer, and the processing cost per case.

## Can a small company start with a PoC?

Yes. The key is not to make the PoC itself the goal.

The advantage of small-scale validation is that before signing a large contract you can confirm not only whether it is technically feasible, but **whether improving that workflow is actually worth it**.

On the other hand, if the requirements are already clear and configuring an existing SaaS product would solve the problem, skipping a custom-development PoC can be the rational choice.

## When is it better not to outsource AI workflow improvement?

In the following cases, it may be better to organize things internally or compare off-the-shelf services before ordering from a development company:

- The workflow to improve has not been decided
- The task is minor and happens only a few times a month
- An existing SaaS product solves it well enough
- The source data is disorganized and the correct answer cannot be defined
- AI errors are unacceptable and there is no way for a person to check the output
- Development and operating costs clearly exceed the savings from automation

Deciding not to adopt AI is also a valid conclusion of a workflow assessment.

## What should you ask before ordering?

You can use these questions as-is in a first consultation:

1. Does this workflow really need AI?
2. What will the first validation measure?
3. Which data will be sent to external services?
4. Where will human review steps remain?
5. Under what conditions will the PoC be judged a failure?
6. If we move to production after the PoC, what gets rebuilt?
7. What is the impact of changing the AI model?
8. If you conclude that we should not build this, will you tell us so?

Whether a partner can answer these concretely reveals differences that a flashy proposal alone will not show.
